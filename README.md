# Symmetry-Resolved Tensor Topology for Computational Oversight of Wildlife Neural Networks

## Spectral Diagonalization, Lie-Algebraic Structure, and Finite Convergence

**Author:** Dr. Bheemaiah Anil K.  
**Affiliation:** Singularity OneProject, Synergy Robotics, Seattle, WA, USA

[📄 Read the full paper (PDF)](symmetry_resolved_tensor_topology_wildlife_ai_arxiv.pdf)

## Overview

This paper proposes **Symmetry-Resolved Tensor Topology (SRTT)** as a framework for auditing the internal computational structure of wildlife-AI neural networks. Rather than evaluating models only through accuracy, F1, mAP, FLOPs, or parameter count, the framework examines spectral organization, symmetry structure, and topological characteristics of learned representations.

The framework is designed for analysis of **ONNX representations, Microsoft MegaDetector, PyTorch-Wildlife, and MegaDetector-Acoustic**.

## Core Framework

### 1. Spectral Diagonalization

For each model tensor \(W_i\), the framework constructs the Hermitian Gram operator

\[
H_i = W_i^\dagger W_i
\]

and applies spectral diagonalization:

\[
H_i = U_i \Lambda_i U_i^\dagger.
\]

The resulting spectrum is used to calculate:

- Spectral entropy
- Effective spectral rank
- Spectral concentration
- Condition-related structural measures

Effective spectral rank distinguishes tensors that may have the same algebraic rank but very different distributions of spectral energy.

### 2. Lie-Algebraic Symmetry

Candidate transformation symmetries are represented using Lie-algebra generators satisfying

\[
[T_a,T_b] = i f_{ab}^{c}T_c.
\]

The framework measures symmetry breaking through the residual

\[
R_a(F)=T'_aF-DFT_a
\]

and aggregates the residual into a symmetry-breaking measure.

For finite or compact symmetry groups, tensor representations can additionally be decomposed into symmetry sectors. **Symmetry leakage** measures the fraction of tensor energy occurring outside symmetry-preserving blocks.

### 3. Tensor Topology

Feature vectors generated by network layers are treated as point clouds. A metric and filtration are constructed, after which **persistent homology** is used to identify structural features across scales.

Persistence provides a complementary descriptor to spectral analysis and parameter counts.

### 4. Symmetry-Resolved Tensor Complexity

The paper proposes the composite metric

\[
C_{STT}(F)
=
\sum_i \omega_i
[
\log(1+r_{eff,i})+
\beta L_{sym,i}
]
+
\gamma\sum_i T_i.
\]

Here:

- \(r_{eff}\) = effective spectral rank
- \(L_{sym}\) = symmetry leakage
- \(T_i\) = topological persistence contribution
- \(\omega_i\) = layer weights
- \(\beta,\gamma\) = application-specific weighting parameters

The metric is intended to add a **structural axis** to conventional model evaluation.

## Main Theoretical Results

### Spectral Bound

For a tensor with rank \(r\),

\[
1 \leq r_{eff}(W) \leq \operatorname{rank}(W).
\]

### Symmetry Leakage Bound

For mutually orthogonal complete symmetry projectors,

\[
0 \leq L_{sym} \leq 1.
\]

### Conditional Monotonicity

For a structure-preserving reduction procedure in which effective spectral rank, symmetry leakage, and topological persistence are nonincreasing,

\[
C_{STT}(F_{k+1}) \leq C_{STT}(F_k).
\]

### Conditional Finite Convergence

If the structural state space is finite or discretized and every non-fixed-point reduction decreases complexity by at least \(\delta>0\), convergence occurs after at most

\[
N \leq
\left\lceil
\frac{C_{STT}(F_0)-C_{min}}{\delta}
\right\rceil
\]

strict reductions.

This is a conditional result for the defined reduction procedure; it does **not** claim convergence of arbitrary neural-network training.

## ONNX Auditing

ONNX is proposed as an auditing representation because its computational graph and tensors can be inspected independently of the original training environment.

For each graph node, the proposed audit extracts:

- Operator type
- Tensor dimensions
- Parameter tensors
- Singular/eigenvalue spectra
- Effective rank
- Candidate symmetry representations
- Symmetry leakage
- Topological descriptors

PyTorch-to-ONNX conversion can then be evaluated by comparing structural audit vectors before and after conversion.

## Wildlife-AI Case Studies

The proposed experimental testbed spans multiple wildlife-monitoring modalities:

- **Microsoft MegaDetector** — camera-trap image detection
- **PyTorch-Wildlife** — wildlife detection and species classification
- **MegaDetector-Acoustic** — bioacoustic wildlife detection and classification

This creates a cross-modal testbed covering visual detection, visual classification, and acoustic classification.

## Experimental Protocol

The proposed experiments will:

1. Acquire released PyTorch and ONNX model artifacts.
2. Extract computational graphs and eligible tensors.
3. Construct and diagonalize \(W_i^\dagger W_i\).
4. Calculate effective rank and spectral entropy.
5. Evaluate candidate symmetry transformations.
6. Calculate symmetry leakage.
7. Construct feature manifolds and persistence diagrams.
8. Calculate the STT complexity metric.
9. Compare structural measurements with accuracy, F1, mAP, parameter count, FLOPs, and latency.
10. Publish model hashes, graph versions, numerical tolerances, symmetry definitions, filtration parameters, and complete outputs for reproducibility.

## Scientific Contribution

The central research question is:

> **Can neural models with comparable predictive performance be distinguished by symmetry-resolved spectral and topological complexity?**

If supported empirically, tensor diagonalization and topological descriptors could provide an additional layer of computational oversight for scientific AI and edge-deployed biodiversity systems.

## Relationship to Quantum Computing

The mathematical construction is inspired by **exact diagonalization and symmetry-sector decomposition** in quantum many-body physics. Neural tensors are treated analogously through spectral diagonalization and symmetry decomposition.

The paper explicitly treats this as a **mathematical analogy**, not a claim that neural networks are quantum Hamiltonians or physical quantum computers.

## Limitations

The manuscript identifies several important limitations:

- Neural networks do not automatically possess exact Lie-group symmetries.
- Topological descriptors depend on feature-space construction, metric, filtration, and sampling.
- Effective rank is representation-dependent.
- Finite-convergence results require a discrete descent condition.
- Composite-metric weights require application-specific calibration.
- Numerical results must be generated from actual released model artifacts.

## Research Status

This repository contains a **methodological preprint draft**. The paper intentionally does not report fabricated experimental measurements.

Numerical spectra, effective ranks, symmetry leakage values, persistence diagrams, and convergence curves should be generated from the actual released model artifacts before empirical publication.
