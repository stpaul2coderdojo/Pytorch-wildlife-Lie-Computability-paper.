# Symmetry-Resolved Tensor Topology for Computational Oversight of Wildlife Neural Networks

**Dr. Bheemaiah Anil K.**\

[📄 Read the full paper

## Overview

This work proposes a **symmetry-resolved tensor-topological framework**
for computational oversight of neural networks used in wildlife
monitoring.

The central idea is to treat a neural network as a composition of tensor
operators and analyze its internal computation through:

-   **spectral diagonalization** of tensor Gram operators,
-   **Lie-algebraic symmetry analysis**,
-   **symmetry-sector decomposition and leakage**,
-   **persistent homology and tensor topology**, and
-   a composite **Symmetry-Resolved Tensor Complexity (STTC)** measure.

The framework is designed for structural auditing of **ONNX**,
**MegaDetector**, **PyTorch-Wildlife**, and **MegaDetector-Acoustic**
model artifacts. The manuscript deliberately does **not** claim
empirical numerical results until the released model artifacts have been
analyzed.

## 1. Mathematical Framework

### 1.1 Neural networks as tensor operators

A trained model is represented as

$$
F_\theta =
T_L \circ T_{L-1} \circ \cdots \circ T_1 ,
$$

where each $T_i$ may represent a parameterized tensor operator,
nonlinear transformation, normalization, convolution, attention
operation, or graph transformation.

Conventional measures such as parameter count, FLOPs, and predictive
accuracy do not uniquely characterize the internal structure of
computation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.2 Lie-algebraic computability

Let $G$ be a transformation group acting on representation space
$\mathcal{H}$ through

$$
\rho:G\rightarrow GL(\mathcal{H}).
$$

For a continuous Lie group, infinitesimal generators satisfy

$$
[T_a,T_b] = i f_{ab}^{\;\;c} T_c .
$$

Exact equivariance is expressed as

$$
F(\rho(g)x)=\rho'(g)F(x).
$$

The corresponding infinitesimal condition is

$$
T'_a F = DF\,T_a ,
$$

where $DF$ is the local derivative.

Define the symmetry residual

$$
R_a(F)=T'_aF-DF\,T_a .
$$

An aggregate symmetry-breaking measure is

$$
R_{\mathrm{sym}}(F)
=
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_a
\left\|R_a(F)\right\|_F^2}
{\displaystyle\sum_a
\left\|T'_aF\right\|_F^2+\varepsilon}.
$$

Thus,

$$
R_{\mathrm{sym}}(F)=0
$$

corresponds to exact equivariance under the tested generators.

> **Interpretation:** "Lie computability" is used operationally to
> measure Lie-algebraic structure; the manuscript does not claim that a
> neural network is itself a physical realization of a particular Lie
> group.

## 2. Spectral Tensor Representation

For each model tensor $W_i$, construct the Hermitian
positive-semidefinite Gram operator

$$
H_i=W_i^\dagger W_i .
$$

By the spectral theorem,

$$
H_i=U_i\Lambda_iU_i^\dagger ,
$$

with non-negative eigenvalues.

Normalize the spectrum:

$$
p_{ij}
=
\frac{\lambda_{ij}}
{\displaystyle\sum_k\lambda_{ik}} .
$$

The spectral entropy is

$$
S_i
=
-\sum_j p_{ij}\log p_{ij}.
$$

The corresponding effective spectral rank is

$$
r_{\mathrm{eff},i}
=
\exp(S_i).
$$

This distinguishes tensors having the same algebraic rank but different
concentrations of spectral energy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Symmetry-Resolved Tensor Decomposition

For a finite or compact symmetry group with representation operators
$U_g$, let $P_\lambda$ denote a projector onto symmetry sector
$\lambda$.

A tensor can be decomposed as

$$
W
=
\sum_{\lambda,\mu}
P_\lambda W P_\mu .
$$

The diagonal blocks represent symmetry-preserving computation, while
off-diagonal blocks couple different symmetry sectors.

Define symmetry leakage by

$$
L_{\mathrm{sym}}
=
\frac{\displaystyle
\sum_{\lambda\neq\mu}
\left\|P_\lambda W P_\mu\right\|_F^2}
{\displaystyle
\left\|W\right\|_F^2+\varepsilon}.
$$

For exact symmetry,

$$
L_{\mathrm{sym}}=0.
$$

The quantity can be interpreted as the fraction of Frobenius energy
outside symmetry-preserving blocks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Tensor Topology

Spectral analysis captures algebraic organization but not necessarily
the topology of learned representations.

Let $X_i$ denote feature vectors generated by a model layer. Construct a
metric $d(x_a,x_b)$ and a filtration

$$
K_\varepsilon(X_i).
$$

Persistent homology computes

$$
H_k(K_\varepsilon)
$$

over filtration scales.

The resulting persistence diagram is

$$
D_k(X_i),
$$

containing birth-death pairs $(b,d)$.

A scalar persistence functional is

$$
T_k(X_i)
=
\sum_{(b,d)\in D_k}
(d-b)^q,
\qquad q\geq1.
$$

This is intended as a structural descriptor rather than a claim that
every neural tensor possesses a representation-independent intrinsic
topology.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Symmetry-Resolved Tensor Complexity

The proposed composite structural metric is

$$
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{STT}}(F)
=
\sum_i\omega_i
\left[
\log(1+r_{\mathrm{eff},i})
+
\beta L_{\mathrm{sym},i}
\right]
+
\gamma\sum_iT_i
}
$$

with

$$
\omega_i\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_i\omega_i=1,
\qquad
\beta,\gamma\geq0.
$$

The metric combines spectral organization, symmetry breaking, and
topological persistence into one structural axis for model oversight.

# 6. Main Theoretical Results

## Proposition 1 --- Spectral Bound

If $W$ has $r$ nonzero singular values, then

$$
\boxed{
1\leq r_{\mathrm{eff}}(W)\leq\operatorname{rank}(W)
}
$$

because the normalized squared singular values form a probability
distribution supported on at most $r$ elements, whose Shannon entropy
lies between $0$ and $\log r$.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Proposition 2 --- Symmetry Leakage Bound

For mutually orthogonal complete projectors,

$$
\|W\|_F^2
=
\sum_{\lambda,\mu}
\|P_\lambda W P_\mu\|_F^2 .
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
0\leq L_{\mathrm{sym}}\leq1
}
$$

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Proposition 3 --- Monotonic Complexity Under Structure-Preserving Reduction

Consider a sequence

$$
F_0,F_1,\ldots,F_N
$$

generated by a model-reduction procedure.

If effective spectral rank, symmetry leakage, and topological
persistence are each nonincreasing at every step, and all metric weights
are nonnegative, then

$$
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{STT}}(F_{k+1})
\leq
C_{\mathrm{STT}}(F_k)
}
$$

The result follows because every constituent quantity contributes
monotonically with a nonnegative weight.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Proposition 4 --- Conditional Finite Convergence

Suppose the reduction procedure operates on a finite or discretized
structural state space and every non-fixed-point reduction decreases
complexity by at least

$$
\delta>0.
$$

If $C_{\min}$ is the minimum attainable complexity, then convergence
occurs after at most

$$
\boxed{
N
\leq
\left\lceil
\frac{
C_{\mathrm{STT}}(F_0)-C_{\min}
}{
\delta
}
\right\rceil
}
$$

strict reductions.

This is a **conditional finite-convergence result for the defined
reduction procedure**; it does not imply convergence of arbitrary
neural-network training.

# 7. ONNX as an Auditing Representation

For each ONNX graph node

$$
v_i:X_i\rightarrow X_{i+1},
$$

the proposed audit extracts:

-   operator type,
-   tensor dimensions,
-   parameter tensors,
-   singular spectrum,
-   effective rank,
-   symmetry representation,
-   symmetry leakage,
-   topological descriptors where applicable.

The resulting structural graph is represented as

$$
G_{\mathrm{ONNX}}=(V,E,S,T),
$$

where $S$ contains spectral quantities and $T$ contains topological
quantities.

A PyTorch-to-ONNX conversion can therefore be evaluated by comparing
structural audit vectors before and after conversion.

# 8. Wildlife-AI Case Studies

The framework is proposed for a cross-modal wildlife-monitoring testbed:

### 🦌 MegaDetector

Camera-trap detection of:

-   animals,
-   people,
-   vehicles.

### 🐾 PyTorch-Wildlife

A broader wildlife-AI framework containing detection models, species
classifiers, and inference tooling.

### 🎧 MegaDetector-Acoustic

Bioacoustic monitoring using:

-   raw audio,
-   Mel-spectrogram generation,
-   neural classification,
-   sliding-window inference.

Together these systems provide a testbed spanning **image detection,
visual classification, and acoustic classification**.

# 9. Experimental Protocol

The proposed empirical workflow is:

1.  Acquire released PyTorch and ONNX model artifacts.
2.  Extract computational graphs and eligible tensors.
3.  Construct $$
    H_i=W_i^\dagger W_i.
    $$
4.  Diagonalize each Gram operator.
5.  Calculate effective rank and spectral entropy.
6.  Calculate condition measures.
7.  Identify candidate symmetry transformations.
8.  Calculate symmetry leakage.
9.  Construct feature manifolds and persistence diagrams.
10. Calculate the STTC metric.
11. Compare structural measurements with:
    -   mAP,
    -   F1,
    -   accuracy,
    -   parameter count,
    -   FLOPs,
    -   latency.
12. Publish model hashes, graph versions, numerical tolerances, symmetry
    definitions, filtration parameters, and complete outputs for
    reproducibility.

The central empirical question is:

> **Can models with comparable predictive performance be distinguished
> by symmetry-resolved spectral and topological complexity?**

# 10. Relationship to Exact Diagonalization and Quantum Computing

The diagonalization procedure is mathematically inspired by exact
diagonalization in quantum many-body computation.

For a quantum Hamiltonian,

$$
H|\psi_n\rangle=E_n|\psi_n\rangle ,
$$

symmetries can be used to decompose the computational space into
invariant sectors.

The neural construction analogously diagonalizes

$$
W^\dagger W
$$

and decomposes tensor spaces into symmetry sectors.

The analogy is **mathematical rather than physical**: the manuscript
does not assert that a neural network is a quantum Hamiltonian.

Representation theory, Lie generators, invariant subspaces, and symmetry
reduction are nevertheless common mathematical structures connecting the
framework to Lie-algebraic quantum computing and exact-diagonalization
methods.

# 11. Scientific Contribution

The proposed framework adds a structural dimension to conventional
neural-network evaluation.

Instead of asking only:

> **Which model performs best?**

it asks:

> **Which model performs well with the most structurally controlled
> computation?**

A model can have excellent predictive performance while exhibiting:

-   concentrated spectra,
-   large condition measures,
-   significant symmetry leakage,
-   unstable internal representations,
-   high topological complexity.

Conversely, a compressed model may retain comparable predictive
performance while reducing structural complexity.

Thus model selection can be formulated as a multi-objective problem
over:

$$
\text{performance}
\quad+\quad
\text{computational cost}
\quad+\quad
\text{structural complexity}.
$$

STTC is intended as an **additional structural oversight axis**, not as
a replacement for accuracy or mAP.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Limitations

The manuscript identifies several important limitations:

-   Neural networks do not automatically possess an exact Lie-group
    symmetry.
-   The framework therefore measures **candidate symmetries**.
-   Topological quantities depend on feature construction, metric,
    filtration, and sampling.
-   Effective rank is representation-dependent.
-   Finite convergence requires a discrete descent condition.
-   STTC weighting parameters require application-specific calibration.
-   Numerical empirical values must be obtained from actual released
    model artifacts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Research Status

This is a **methodological preprint draft**.

It intentionally does **not** report fabricated experimental
measurements. Numerical spectra, effective ranks, symmetry leakage
values, persistence diagrams, and convergence curves should be generated
from the actual released model artifacts before submission as an
empirical paper.

This distinction is intentional and is part of the reproducibility
standard of the work.
